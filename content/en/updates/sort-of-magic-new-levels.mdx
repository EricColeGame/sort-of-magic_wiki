## Level Archetypes You'll Meet in the New Boards

Most sorting-puzzle updates recycle a small set of archetypes and remix them. Recognizing which one you're looking at in the first five seconds is the single biggest time-saver in the game.

| Archetype | Core Mechanic | Difficulty | Best Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open sort | Plenty of free space | Low | Work in clean groups; don't scatter pieces |
| Cluttered sort | Most slots blocked at start | Medium | Clear blockers before sorting anything |
| Timed sort | Move or time limit | Medium–High | Plan the full sequence before tapping |
| Locked piece | Some items can't move until freed | High | Prioritize whatever unlocks the cluster |
| Multi-goal | Two or more objectives at once | High | Sequence goals so one move serves both |

If you only remember one row, remember "locked piece." Community reports describe these as the stages where casual play stops working, because a single overlooked lock can freeze an entire quadrant of the board.

## Tips and Strategies for Clearing New Levels Faster

These tactics are general enough to survive any specific update, and specific enough to be useful tonight.

- **Read the board before your first move.** Ten seconds of scanning beats ten moves of guessing.
- **Work from the edges inward.** Corners and borders are usually the most constrained slots.
- **Batch your goals.** In multi-goal stages, find the move that advances two objectives at once.
- **Don't hoard forever.** Boosters exist to break deadlocks, not to be admired.
- **Restart early.** If the opening layout is terrible, a restart costs less than a rescue.
- **Play one stage at a time.** Chain-playing new content leads to sloppy taps.

| Booster | Best Used When | Avoid When |
|---|---|---|
| Shuffle / reorder | Board is locked but you still have moves | You have an obvious move available |
| Extra moves | You're one or two moves from a clear | You've barely started |
| Single-piece clear | One stubborn piece blocks everything | Multiple blockers remain |
| Time extension | Timed stages, near the end | Untimed stages (wasted) |

A good rule of thumb: never spend a booster in the first third of a stage. Most boards that feel impossible at move five become solvable by move fifteen, simply because the clutter clears itself as you sort.

### When to Walk Away

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are the sort of magic new levels free to play?**

In most games of this type, new stages are added to the existing game at no extra cost, with optional boosters or in-app purchases layered on top. Exact pricing and availability depend on your platform and region, so check your app store listing for the current details.

**How long does it take to finish the new levels?**

It varies enormously with skill and how often you replay. A casual player working through a few boards per session might take a couple of weeks; a dedicated player pushing straight through could clear the main list in a few sittings. The gatekeeper stages near the end will slow almost everyone down.

**Do I need to finish old levels before starting the new ones?**

Not usually — most updates simply extend the stage list. But finishing any half-completed old boards first keeps the rules fresh in your mind and avoids confusion if the update tweaked an existing mechanic.

**What's the best booster for the hardest new stages?**

Extra moves and shuffles tend to be the most flexible, because they solve the two most common deadlocks: running out of moves and running out of options. Save both for the final few boards rather than spending them early.

**Why don't I see the new levels yet?**

Rollouts are staged. Update the app manually, close and reopen it, and check again in a few hours. If your app store shows no update available, the release simply hasn't reached your region or device yet — a normal part of how mobile updates ship.
